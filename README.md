## Overview

The Cash–Landrum incident took place on the evening of December 29, 1980, on a rural two-lane road (FM 1485 / 2100) in dense woods near Dayton, Texas, in the Houston exurbs. Betty Cash (age 51), Vickie Landrum (57), and Landrum's seven-year-old grandson Colby reported that as they returned home from a restaurant they encountered a large, diamond-shaped craft with a flat top and bottom, hovering above the road and emitting a downward cone of flame so intense that it heated the metal of their car. The craft was, according to the witnesses, escorted by approximately 23 helicopters in tight formation, including identifiable Boeing CH-47 Chinooks. In the days that followed, all three witnesses reported nausea, vomiting, headaches, sunburn-like skin reactions, blistering, and hair loss; Cash, who had spent the longest time outside the car, was hospitalized in a critical condition. The case became one of the most-cited 'physical effects' UFO encounters of the 20th century and is unusual for the substantial primary documentation of the witnesses' medical condition. Cash and Landrum sued the U.S. government for $20 million; the case was dismissed in 1986 by U.S. District Court Judge Ross Sterling on the basis that the United States neither owned the object nor operated the helicopters described. The principal investigator was MUFON's John F. Schuessler, an aerospace engineer at NASA's Johnson Space Center; the U.S. Army's own investigation was conducted by Lt. Col. George Sarran. Skeptics including Philip J. Klass, Curt Collins, and Brian Dunning have argued that the medical evidence does not require a UFO explanation and have raised questions about the timeline and corroboration.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Misidentified secret aircraft** _(John F. Schuessler, various MUFON investigators)_

Hypothesis that the object was an experimental U.S. military or contractor aircraft (variously suggested as a nuclear test platform or vectored-thrust prototype), with an associated helicopter recovery escort.

**Genuine non-conventional craft** _(MUFON, Stanton Friedman (in commentary))_

Hypothesis that the object was a non-human or otherwise non-conventional craft, with the helicopters part of a recovery or monitoring response.

### Skeptical

**Misidentification and unrelated illness** _(Philip J. Klass, Brian Dunning, Curt Collins)_

Hypothesis that the witnesses encountered an unrelated aircraft event (possibly a flare or industrial flame) and that the medical complaints, while real, are not attributable to a UFO encounter.

**Federal court findings** _(U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Texas)_

U.S. District Court Judge Sterling found in 1986 that the witnesses had not produced evidence that any U.S. agency owned the craft or operated the helicopters.
